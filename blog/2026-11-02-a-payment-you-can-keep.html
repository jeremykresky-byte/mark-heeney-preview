A payment you can make is not the same as a payment you can keep

November 2, 2026 · Mark Heeney, Mortgage Agent Level 2

A lender’s maximum is a ceiling, not a recommendation. A payment you can make on paper can still fail once groceries, daycare, a car repair, and a slow paycheque land in the same month.

Why this matters now

Pre-approvals and renewal offers both quote a number. The useful question is whether that number leaves room for the life already in the bank statements.

A practical checklist

  • Start from take-home pay, not gross pay.
  • Subtract the proposed housing payment, including property tax and heat if those are not already in it.
  • Subtract debt payments that will still exist after the new mortgage.
  • Subtract the spending that showed up in the last three months: food, fuel, insurance, childcare, and the irregular bills.
  • If the remainder cannot absorb one ordinary surprise, the payment is too tight even if a lender would allow it.

Common mistakes

  • Using a pre-approval amount as a target price for the house.
  • Ignoring debts you plan to “pay off at closing” unless that payoff is actually in the numbers.
  • Counting overtime you have not received for several months.

Where a mortgage conversation fits

A good mortgage conversation starts with the payment you can keep, then looks for a structure that fits it. That may mean a different price, a different down payment, or waiting. It is a better outcome than a payment that only works on the application.
