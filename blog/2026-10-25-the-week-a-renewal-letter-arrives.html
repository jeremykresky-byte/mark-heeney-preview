The week a renewal letter arrives

October 25, 2026 · Mark Heeney, Mortgage Agent Level 2

A renewal letter is an offer from the lender you already have. It is not a survey of the market, and it is not a verdict that your payment must rise by exactly that amount. The useful work in the first week is reading the offer and reading your own month.

Why this matters now

Payments reset. Households that wait until the last days lose the time to compare a structure, gather documents, or decide that staying is actually the better deal.

A practical checklist

  • Write down the offered rate, the term, the payment, and the maturity date. Note whether the payment includes tax.
  • Put that payment into this month’s budget in place of the old one. See what breaks.
  • List debts you might clear or consolidate at the same time, and the penalty if you leave early. Do not assume there is no penalty.
  • Gather a recent mortgage statement, proof of income, and property tax. Comparison is slow when the paperwork starts late.
  • Decide what you need from a conversation: a lower payment, a shorter amortization, or confirmation that the letter is already fair.

Common mistakes

  • Signing because the form is familiar.
  • Chasing a slightly lower rate that adds a restriction or a fee you did not price.
  • Waiting until the renewal week to discover a credit card the budget cannot carry beside the new payment.

Where a mortgage conversation fits

Bring the letter. A mortgage agent can tell you whether shopping is worth it on this file, or whether the offer in your hand is the one to keep. Mark has also written about renewal payments on the MPFG site if you want the longer version before you book.
