Budgeting

A debt payoff order you can explain without a spreadsheet

October 23, 2026 · Mark Heeney, Mortgage Agent Level 2

Extra money pays off debt faster only if it is aimed at one balance. Sprinkling it across every bill feels fair and finishes nothing. You need an order you can say in one sentence.

Why this matters now

Before anyone talks about folding debt into a mortgage, it helps to know what a focused payoff would do on its own. Sometimes that plan is enough. Sometimes it shows why the timeline is too long.

A practical checklist

  • List every non-mortgage debt with its balance, rate, and minimum payment.
  • Avalanche: point every extra dollar at the highest rate first. This usually costs less interest.
  • Snowball: point every extra dollar at the smallest balance first. This finishes an account sooner, which some people will actually stick with.
  • Pick one. Pay minimums on the rest. When an account is gone, move its whole payment to the next account.
  • Do not refinance the mortgage in the same week you invent this order unless the payment crisis is already here. Give the order a fair look first.

Common mistakes

  • Changing the order every payday.
  • Paying extra on the mortgage while a much higher-rate card is growing.
  • Using a windfall on the smallest bill for the feeling of it, then putting new purchases back on that card.

Where a mortgage conversation fits

If the order you can afford still leaves high-interest debt in place for many years, bring the list to a mortgage meeting and ask what consolidation would change. Include the risk of securing that debt against the house. The order is still useful after a consolidation, because new balances need a rule.
