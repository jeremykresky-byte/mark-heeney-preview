Money management

Pay yourself first, after the bills that cannot slip

October 28, 2026 · Mark Heeney, Mortgage Agent Level 2

“Pay yourself first” is a good habit and a bad slogan if the mortgage or the minimum payments are the thing that slips. Savings that cause a missed housing payment are not a plan. They are a shuffle.

Why this matters now

Automatic transfers feel responsible. They are responsible when they come after the obligations that protect the house and the credit file.

A practical checklist

  • List the transfers that must clear: housing, utilities, insurance, and contractual debt payments.
  • Schedule those first, on the day income lands.
  • Schedule a savings transfer the same day, for an amount that still leaves the flexible spending cap intact.
  • If the savings transfer causes overdraft, lower it. A small transfer that survives is better than a large one you reverse.
  • Raise the savings amount only after two months of no bounced payments.

Common mistakes

  • Saving into an investment while a 20 percent card balance sits untouched and growing.
  • Using the emergency fund as the “savings” and then spending it on ordinary shortfalls.
  • Automating a transfer you have never test-run against a real payday.

Where a mortgage conversation fits

Extra mortgage prepayments are a form of paying yourself, and they are optional. They come after cash reserves and after high-interest debt you cannot deduct or easily refinance. If you want the prepayment to be the savings plan, check the privilege and the penalty before you set it up.
