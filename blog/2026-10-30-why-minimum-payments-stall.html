Debt consolidation

Why credit card minimums stall a payoff

October 30, 2026 · Mark Heeney, Mortgage Agent Level 2

A minimum payment is designed to keep the account current. It is a weak plan for becoming debt-free. A large share of it can be interest, so the balance barely moves while the card still feels “handled.”

Why this matters now

People consolidate because the minimums added together feel impossible, not because they have priced the interest. Seeing the timeline first tells you whether you need a new product or a different payment.

A practical checklist

  • On each card, write the balance, the rate, and this month’s minimum.
  • Ask the issuer, or use its statement disclosure, how long the balance lasts if you pay only the minimum.
  • Pick one card and add a fixed extra amount you can repeat. Even a modest extra payment changes the timeline more than skipping a latte and calling it a plan.
  • Stop adding new purchases to any card you are trying to pay off. A payoff and new spending on the same card cancel each other.
  • If the honest timeline is many years, then price a consolidation. Include the fee, the new rate, and whether the debt would be secured by your home.

Common mistakes

  • Opening a new card to “transfer the problem” without a payoff date.
  • Paying a little extra on every card and finishing none of them.
  • Ignoring the interest line on the statement because the account is not past due.

Where a mortgage conversation fits

A mortgage refinance is one consolidation tool, not the first one to assume. If a short, unsecured plan can clear the cards, leaving the house out of it is often the smaller risk. If the minimums are already colliding with the mortgage, bring both sets of numbers to the meeting.
